# Fictional AI decision example: library outreach

This short example shows the structure of a bounded decision. It is not a completed
retail assignment or evidence from a deployed system. All details below are fictional.

**Observation:** a small library trial finds that an outreach rule misses some
patrons needing accessible services. The sample does not represent all branches.

**Decision:** defer automated allocation. Offer voluntary human-reviewed outreach
while collecting purpose-limited evaluation evidence. The library service lead
owns approval; the data steward reviews collection and access. Avoid collecting
medical details simply because they might help prediction.

**Transparency and review:** tell patrons why they received an invitation, make
participation optional, and provide a human contact to correct information. Review
missed-service complaints before extending the trial. Retain only records needed
for the stated trial purpose under an approved schedule; that schedule and deletion
checks remain proposed, not demonstrated.

**Framework connection:** Govern assigns approval and stewardship. Map identifies
exclusion harm and the service context. Measure asks whether evidence represents
branches and affected patrons. Manage defers automatic allocation and sets review.

**Changed purpose:** a request to reuse outreach scores to restrict borrowing
would require a new decision. Pause that proposed use, seek evidence and affected-
patron input, and revise the notice/appeal design before approval. Do not assume
that outreach evidence supports restrictions.

Why this helps: the response ties one decision to evidence, affected people,
accountable roles and a limit. In your retail worksheet, apply this structure to
your actual metrics and scenario; do not copy these fictional observations.
